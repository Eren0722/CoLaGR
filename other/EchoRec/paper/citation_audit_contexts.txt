However, the sequential space and the LLM semantic space obey different neighborhood rules. The former reflects ID co-occurrence, popularity bias, and sparse interaction noise, whereas the latter is organized by textual semantics and world knowledge~\cite{lin2025survey}. We refer to this gap as the Semantic-Sequential Heterogeneity Gap (SSHG). SSHG mainly appears in two forms that matter for injection: local semantic metric mismatch, where semantic neighbors in the frozen LLM space are not preserved in the raw SRec space, and semantic-neighborhood topological fragmentation, where semantically similar users or sequences fail to form coherent local regions. Recent work on token-level collaborative alignment points to incompatible local rules across these spaces~\cite{lin2026tca4rec}. LLM-based view augmentation and cross-modal contrastive learning report related mismatches from different angles~\cite{lu2025cllmrec,xu2024cmclrec}. As a result, a lightweight projector may weaken transferable sequential cues when forced to denoise, reorganize local structure, and align the sequential space at once. Recent diagnosis reports the same symptom: LLM-based recommenders may rely on shortcut context rather than stable sequential dependence~\cite{kim2025lost,wang2026contextbias}. Reasoning-oriented recommendation studies also suggest that shallow reasoning can obscure whether sequential preference is truly captured~\cite{yu2026thinkrec}.

From a cross-space transfer viewpoint, effective injection requires a teacher space whose local geometry is compatible with the target semantic host. Inspired by recent large-small collaboration studies in recommendation~\cite{zhang2026crossscale,lv2025lsc4rec}, we propose EchoRec, a staged large-small collaborative recommendation framework organized around reshape first, inject later. EchoRec first uses a frozen LLM to construct offline semantic assets from items and user histories, and then applies Semantic-Aligned Contrastive Pre-training (SACP) to the sequential teacher under next-item supervision together with item-level and user-level semantic constraints, reducing local semantic metric mismatch and semantic-neighborhood fragmentation in the teacher space. Sequence Injection (SI) then projects the purified teacher item embeddings into the frozen LLM and aligns LLM-side user states with teacher representations, transferring refined sequential structure with lower cross-space distortion.

With the rise of large language models, recommendation research has explored tighter ways of coupling LLMs with sequential signals~\cite{lin2025survey,zhao2024era}. Early efforts mainly adapted LLMs through prompting or lightweight tuning: TALLRec~\cite{bao2023tallrec} uses PEFT to bridge language modeling and recommendation, while ThinkRec~\cite{yu2026thinkrec} further introduces explicit reasoning. As purely textual adaptation showed clear limits, later work brought collaborative knowledge back into the LLM pipeline. LLaRA~\cite{liao2024llara} combines CF-SRec item embeddings with textual prompts, CoLLM~\cite{zhang2025collm} directly injects collaborative embeddings into the online LLM context, and A-LLMRec~\cite{kim2024allmrec} further strengthens CF knowledge absorption through representation alignment. More recently, Kim et al.~\cite{kim2025lost} show that existing LLM4Rec models still fail to capture dynamic preference from interaction sequences, and introduce LLM-SRec for sequence-aware representation distillation. Another line uses LLMs to enhance smaller recommenders, including RLMRec~\cite{ren2024rlmrec}, LRD~\cite{yang2024lrd}, LLM-ESR~\cite{liu2024llmesr}, SRA-CL~\cite{cui2025sracl}, and LLMEmb~\cite{liu2025llmemb}, while TCA4Rec~\cite{lin2026tca4rec} explores more explicit collaborative alignment in the generative space. Inspired by recent large-small collaboration studies~\cite{zhang2026crossscale,lv2025lsc4rec}, we argue that these two directions should not be treated separately. Nevertheless, existing methods mainly focus on alignment or distillation at injection time, and pay much less attention to the structural mismatch between the teacher space and the LLM semantic host before injection.
